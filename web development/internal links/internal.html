Input devices

  1. Mouse - A handheld pointing device used to move a cursor on a computer screen. Modern mice typically use optical or laser technology to track movement and can be connected via a wire or wirelessly. They usually feature at least two buttons and a scroll wheel for easy navigation.
  2. Keyboard - A set of keys used to input text and commands into a computer. Keyboards come in various layouts, such as QWERTY, and types, including mechanical and membrane. Ergonomic keyboards are designed to reduce strain, while gaming keyboards often include extra programmable keys.
  3. Touchpad - A touch-sensitive surface used on laptops as a substitute for a mouse. It allows users to control the cursor with their finger. Many touchpads also support multi-touch gestures, such as pinching to zoom or using two fingers to scroll.
  4. Scanner - A device that optically scans images, printed text, or objects and converts them to a digital image. Flatbed scanners are common for documents and photos, while sheet-fed scanners are faster for multi-page documents.
  5. Microphone - A device that converts sound waves into an electrical signal to be used by a computer. Microphones are essential for voice chat, recording audio, and using voice command software. They come in various forms, including standalone desktop models and those built into headsets or webcams.
  6. Webcam - A video camera that feeds or streams its image in real time to or through a computer to a computer network. Webcams are widely used for video conferencing, online streaming, and creating video content. The quality is often measured by resolution and frame rate.

Output devices

  1. Monitor - A screen that displays visual output from a computer. Monitors use various technologies like LCD, LED, and OLED, and come in different resolutions, such as 1080p, 1440p, and 4K, which affect image clarity. Refresh rate is another key factor, especially for gaming.
  2. Printer - A device that produces a hard copy (text or graphics) of documents stored in electronic form. The most common types are inkjet printers, which spray ink onto paper, and laser printers, which use toner. 3D printers are a newer type that create three-dimensional objects.
  3. Speaker - A device that converts an electrical audio signal into a corresponding sound. Computer speakers can be simple stereo pairs or more complex surround sound systems (e.g., 5.1 or 7.1) for an immersive audio experience.
  4. Projector - A device that projects an image (or moving images) onto a surface, commonly a projection screen. Projectors are used in classrooms, business presentations, and home theaters. They use technologies like DLP or LCD to create the image.
  5. Headphones - A pair of small speakers worn on or around the head over a user's ears. They provide a personal listening experience. Types include over-ear, on-ear, and in-ear, with some models offering features like noise cancellation for use in loud environments.
  6. Plotter - A specialized printer that creates high-quality graphics by drawing lines with pens. Plotters are often used for technical drawings, such as architectural blueprints and engineering designs, where precision and large format are required.
